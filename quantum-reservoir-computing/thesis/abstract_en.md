# Abstract

Recent research suggests that extending the reservoir computing paradigm, long established in machine learning, to quantum systems is a promising approach to time-series prediction and complex pattern recognition. This thesis presents a quantum reservoir model designed to offer an efficient implementation while addressing the practical requirements of execution on physical quantum hardware.

The model was implemented using the Qiskit quantum computing framework, which supports both quantum circuit simulation and execution on real quantum devices. The system investigated in this thesis is a six-qubit quantum reservoir whose internal dynamics are governed by discrete-time evolution under an Ising-type Hamiltonian. The input signal, generated in this case by a logistic map, is encoded in the evolution time of the system, thereby reducing the complexity of the input interface.

Information about the quantum states is extracted through measurements in three Pauli bases (\(Z\), \(X\), and \(Y\)). The resulting expectation values are used to construct a high-dimensional, nonlinear feature space, on which a classical linear regression model is trained to predict the time series.

The main contribution of this work is an approach in which input information is introduced into the quantum reservoir not through explicit encoding operations, but by dynamically varying the time parameter governing the system's evolution. By eliminating the need for a separate input-encoding unit, this approach simplifies the reservoir architecture and facilitates its physical implementation.

The model is evaluated on a time series generated by a logistic map. The low prediction error achieved demonstrates the model's effectiveness on this task. The sensitivity of prediction accuracy to various model parameters is also investigated. Performance is found to depend on the number of qubits, the number of measurements, and the complexity of the quantum circuit. Accuracy is further improved by combining measurement bases and incorporating the squares of the expectation values as additional features.

The model is suitable for evaluation on real quantum hardware, as all its components can be implemented on quantum devices available through IBM Quantum. The use of physically motivated design choices, including low circuit depth and local interactions, further supports its feasibility on current quantum technologies. These properties suggest promising directions for future research.
